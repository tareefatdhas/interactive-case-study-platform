# Classfully article standard

## Voice

Write like an experienced instructor helping another instructor prepare for next week.

- Use plain language.
- Use active voice.
- Name the classroom situation early.
- Prefer a concrete example over a broad claim.
- Repeat a clear term instead of rotating through synonyms.
- Keep paragraphs short enough to scan on a phone.
- Use "students" and "instructors," not users, participants, or facilitators unless the distinction is necessary.

## Avoid AI writing patterns

- No em dashes.
- No "not only X, but also Y" constructions.
- No "it is not just X, it is Y."
- No forced sets of three.
- No repeated summary of the introduction.
- No inflated transitions such as additionally, moreover, furthermore, or in conclusion.
- No unsupported "research shows" or "experts say."
- No claims that a tool transforms, revolutionizes, or unlocks teaching.

## Article shape

The structure should fit the reader's question. Do not force identical headings across every article.

Most practical guides should include:

1. A direct answer within the first 150 words
2. A classroom decision or useful filter
3. A practical sequence, schedule, or example
4. Considerations for a large university class
5. A section connecting the live moment to course progress
6. A specific next action

## Conversion design

Use two or three CTAs only when each one fits the surrounding content.

- Early CTA: let an instructor try the workflow after the direct answer
- Middle CTA: connect a relevant product capability to the method being explained
- Final CTA: help the instructor create a class or prepare a session

Do not interrupt every section with a signup request. Do not use fake scarcity, countdowns, or unsupported social proof.

## Search quality

- One primary query per article
- Secondary phrases that express the same intent
- A title that promises the exact answer delivered
- A description that explains the practical outcome
- Descriptive headings written for readers
- Internal links where the next resource genuinely helps
- Article schema, canonical URL, large image preview, and sitemap entry

## Visual standard

Images should show the relationship between students, the instructor, and the shared classroom signal.

Use:

- University settings and adult students
- Warm paper, ink navy, violet, coral, mint, and sun yellow
- Tactile editorial depth and restrained hand-made energy
- One purposeful visual path or focal idea
- Clear composition at thumbnail size

Avoid:

- Text embedded in the image
- Random floating shapes
- Confetti without a meaningful event
- School children
- Generic corporate teams
- Fake dashboards or unreadable interface text
- Sci-fi screens and holograms

Every image needs useful alt text that describes the subject and purpose, not its art style.

## Pre-review checklist

- Does this answer a real instructor question?
- Does the answer appear early?
- Could a reader use something from it without buying Classfully?
- Are all changing facts cited and checked?
- Are product claims true in the current app?
- Is the article distinct from existing coverage?
- Does the CTA fit what the reader is ready to do?
- Does it read naturally aloud?
- Is every section necessary?
- Does the mobile layout remain easy to scan?
